How the peg pyramid works

The board is a triangle of staggered pegs, with each row one peg wider than the row above it. A ball released at the top has an even chance of slipping left or right, then repeats that 50/50 split at each row. After crossing the full board, it lands in one of the pockets at the bottom — always one more pocket than the number of rows.

Because every path is made of random left-right turns, the middle pockets are hit most often while the outer pockets are rare. Mathematicians call the shape a Galton board, and the result it creates is a binomial distribution. In plain terms, the payout table is built around that curve: common centre pockets pay less, and the hard-to-hit edges pay more.

Multipliers, odds, and the house margin

Each pocket value is shaped by its chance of being hit, then trimmed a little for the house margin.
